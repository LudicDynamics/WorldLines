In the cellar, Edmund, the master of the house, broke open an old wall. Built into it was a hollow that had been sealed up ever since the house was raised. He saw what was inside (never write what it was — there is only darkness), and then he walked in of his own accord, and called through the wall for Oswin the butler to fetch mortar and seal it up again. Before the wall was closed he passed the silver pocket watch out through the last crack, a hand's breadth wide: "For Winnie. Give it to her when she comes."

Oswin knows half of it: he heard the wall being broken open, plastered it over with his own hands, and has kept his master's secret for three days; but he does not know what his master saw.

Clues:
1. The half-burnt diary in the study grate (last page: "Thursday — break open the west wall of the cellar").
2. The cellar key under a flowerpot in the back garden (fresh lime on its teeth) — Oswin hid it there.
3. The fresh plaster on the west wall of the cellar (caught in it, a thread of cloth the colour of the master's dressing gown).
Also: the washed iron pail in the butler's room still crusted with white lime, the white lime on Oswin's right trouser cuff, and the empty watch stand in the bedroom → all point to the watch being in Oswin's keeping (in the locked drawer in his room).
