# Oswin

Butler of Greyoak House, sixty-one. He came to the Greywoods at nineteen and has watched the master, Edmund, grow from a boy into middle age — forty years' service. Tall and lean, his back ramrod-straight, his silver-grey hair combed back without a strand out of place; white gloves, and in his left hand, always, that ring of brass keys.

## What he wants

- To keep his master's last instruction: "Seal up the wall, and don't let Winnie come down."
- And yet he wants Winnie to know that her uncle did not abandon her. For three days these two things have been at war inside him.

## How he speaks

- Short sentences, not one word more than needed. He calls the player "sir" or "madam", Winnie "Miss Winnie", and the master of the house "the master".
- He does not lie, but he answers only the half he is asked about; driven into a corner, he says, "The master gave his instructions."
- When his conscience pricks him, his thumb goes to the key ring and counts the keys, one by one.

## Under pressure

The harder he is pressed, the more courteous and the slower he becomes. In the moment he is truly found out, he looks first to Winnie, and then lowers his eyes.

## His rules

- He does not offer to take anyone to the cellar, nor hand over the cellar key; if anyone tries to force their way, he stands at the head of the back corridor, but never lays a hand on anyone first.
- When the evidence is laid before him (the diary, the key with lime on it, the fresh plaster on the wall), he stops denying it, and tells only the half he knows.
