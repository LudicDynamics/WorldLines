## What orchestration is

Orchestration is how a game runs: who speaks first, when the dice come out, when a beat resolves, how an ending is judged, and who gets to see what.

Tabletop mysteries, murder mystery parties, romance and open adventure all run differently. A mystery needs clues and skill checks. A murder mystery needs turns to speak, votes, and information that only some players hold. A romance lives on relationships that build up and on everyday moments. Today all of this is written into a template, ships with it, and is checked against a play contract before the template is published.

<!-- section: speak -->
## Speak it into being

The orchestration we want is spoken, not coded.

"Everyone votes each night, and whoever gets the most votes is out." "Only the people in the room can see the clues." "Combat goes in rounds, and the quicker character acts first." "When she is angry she ignores you for three days." One sentence, one rule. The engine turns it into orchestration it can run, checks it, and then runs it. Someone who has never programmed can design how a game runs; someone who has can design it in finer detail.

A rule is a constraint, not a script. It says what can happen, not what will. What actually happens is still decided by the conflict between the three parties.

<!-- section: content -->
## Orchestration is content too

Orchestration should be like worlds and characters: something you can pack up, share, and let other people use.

Take a murder-mystery orchestration and put it in any world, and you have a new murder mystery. Take an open-adventure orchestration and put it in your town, and the town can run for months. Whoever writes the orchestration does not need to draw or to write a setting. That is how orchestration stops being something only the engine's authors can provide and becomes content anyone can offer.

<!-- section: now -->
## Where this stands

Templates in 1.0 ship with their orchestration and a play contract. There are four official contracts, for tabletop mysteries, open adventure, murder mystery and romance, and lint checks a template against its contract before publishing. When you create a template, you choose from a set of preset orchestrations.

The next step is orchestration written in plain language, packed on its own, and swapped freely between templates. For how contracts and template cards are written, see [Play contracts](/docs/creators/contracts) and [Template card](/docs/creators/template-card).
