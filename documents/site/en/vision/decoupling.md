## A character does not belong to any one world

The character library stands on its own. A world holds links to characters, not the characters themselves. One character can enter many worlds and takes its memories along: the people it met and the things it did in one world, it still remembers in the next.

A character's author only has to make the character: personality, way of speaking, lines it will not cross, and a past. SillyTavern character cards import directly. See [Your character](/docs/players/your-character).

<!-- section: world -->
## A world is a setting plus assets

A world has two layers. The setting is its places, relationships, time, rules and events. The assets are portraits, scene art, music, and the images the interface uses. Both layers are data files, and each can be swapped, translated or extended on its own.

Change the portraits and it is still the same world. Translate it into another language and the rules are still the same rules. Artists, writers and translators each do their own part without waiting on one another.

<!-- section: play -->
## Play is a layer of its own

Play is the rules pack, the play contract and the orchestration: how checks are made, how a beat resolves, how a game runs. It is not tied to any one setting.

The same town can host a tabletop mystery or a murder mystery. The same murder-mystery rules can move to a royal court or an expedition. Change the play, and the setting and assets stay as they are.

<!-- section: why -->
## Why we split it this way

Everyone makes only the layer they are good at. Artists make assets, writers make settings, rules people make play, and character authors make characters. The engine assembles them into a game according to the specs.

The specs are public; see [Specs](/docs/developers/specs). Templates, characters and orchestration can all be packed up and taken along, or published for others to use. Splitting things apart is how more people can take part in building a world, and how one world can be recombined again and again and keep growing for a long time.
