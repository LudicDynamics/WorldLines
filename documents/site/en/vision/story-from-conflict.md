## Three parties, each holding its ground

Every game in WorldLines has three parties, and none of them speaks for the others.

- **The world**, played by the host. Places, time, prices and rules belong to it. Whether something can happen, and whether dice are rolled, is its call, and once it decides, the result is written into the world.
- **The characters**, the agents who live there. Each has goals, memories and lines it will not cross. A character can agree or refuse, and when you are not around it goes about its own plans.
- **The player**, which is you. What you say is an attempt, not something that has already happened.

The three are kept apart. Words you put in a character's mouth do not count, a character cannot change the world's rules, and the world never makes up a character's mind for it.

<!-- section: conflict -->
## How conflict turns into drama

What a character wants and what the world allows rarely line up. What you want and what a character wants rarely line up either. Events come from exactly those gaps.

Every turn has two beats: planning first, then resolution. During planning the host sets the scene, the characters present say where they stand, and the world holds still. Once the talk is settled, the host writes down what happened in this beat, rolls whatever dice are due, and the result stays in the world. The dice have the final word; the narration cannot overrule them. Nothing written into the world is taken back. The game only goes forward.

So nobody knows how a game will end, not even the person who built the world.

<!-- section: script -->
## Why we call it writing a story

A game you have played is already a script: events, dialogue, choices and an ending, recorded in order in a single ledger. Saves, branches and replays all read from that ledger. You can go back to a turn and take a different path, or export the whole game for someone else to read.

Generative AI gives us endless stories. What we want are stories that never have to end: one world keeps running, and new scripts keep growing out of it.

<!-- section: engine -->
## What the engine is for

NeonRP is the engine that makes all of this happen: world state, character agents, turns, the judge and the ledger. You bring the world and the characters. It sets them against each other and writes down everything that follows. It does not write the story. It lets the story happen.
